<h3 id="bank-mocks-h74">M2-Q03 (V2-D1.2)</h3>

<p id="sec-bank-mocks-p327" data-kind="prose"><strong>Scenario.</strong> A hiring pipeline runs: resume intake, validation, skill
extraction, model ranking, verification, interview invite, feedback. The
HR database is the system of record. A wrong invite wastes interviewer
time but is reversible.</p>

<p id="sec-bank-mocks-p328" data-kind="prose"><strong>Question.</strong> Where does verification sit, and at what strength?</p>

<p id="sec-bank-mocks-p329" data-kind="prose"><strong>Options.</strong>
A) After ranking and before the invite: check the extracted skills
against the source resume in code. Lightweight, since the invite is
reversible.
B) After the invite is sent.
C) Before intake.
D) Nowhere.</p>

<p id="sec-bank-mocks-p330" data-kind="prose"><strong>Key.</strong> A. Decisive phrase: "wrong invite wastes interviewer time but
is reversible." Why: verification sits after the model and before the
side effect. Reversibility keeps it lightweight: a code check, not a
human gate. Counterfactual: a human gate wins if the invite were a
binding job offer.</p>


<h3 id="bank-mocks-h75">M2-Q04 (V2-D1.2), Select TWO</h3>

<p id="sec-bank-mocks-p331" data-kind="prose"><strong>Scenario.</strong> A payment pipeline takes a chat request. The pipeline validates the request. The pipeline checks the ledger. The ledger is the system of record. The pipeline runs a fraud model.</p>

<p id="sec-bank-mocks-p332" data-kind="prose">The pipeline executes the transfer. You can manipulate chat input. The ledger is a trusted system.</p>

<p id="sec-bank-mocks-p333" data-kind="prose"><strong>Question.</strong> Which TWO are trust boundaries? Select TWO.</p>

<p id="sec-bank-mocks-p334" data-kind="prose"><strong>Options.</strong>
A) Between chat input and validation.
B) Between the fraud model and the transfer execution.
C) Between the ledger read and the fraud model.
D) Between two internal services with mutual TLS in one VPC.
E) Between the log writer and disk.</p>

<p id="sec-bank-mocks-p335" data-kind="prose"><strong>Answer.</strong> A, B</p>

<p id="sec-bank-mocks-p336" data-kind="prose"><strong>Key.</strong> A, B. Decisive phrase: "Chat input may be manipulated... runs
a fraud model, and executes the transfer." Why: untrusted chat crosses
into validation (screen it). The transfer is the side effect, so the
model-to-transfer crossing needs authorization in code. C, D, and E are
internal trusted crossings. Counterfactual: D wins if the services sit
in different trust zones.</p>


<h3 id="bank-mocks-h76">M2-Q05 (V2-D1.3)</h3>

<p id="sec-bank-mocks-p337" data-kind="prose"><strong>Scenario.</strong> A newsroom drafts market summaries. The steps are fixed. First, fetch prices. Second, compute changes. Third, draft.</p>

<p id="sec-bank-mocks-p338" data-kind="prose">Fourth, an editor checks. Fifth, publish. About 10% of drafts need judgment for ambiguous events.</p>

<p id="sec-bank-mocks-p339" data-kind="prose"><strong>Question.</strong> Which pattern fits?</p>

<p id="sec-bank-mocks-p340" data-kind="prose"><strong>Options.</strong>
A) Pure agent loop for everything.
B) Deterministic workflow for the fixed steps with an agent call routed
in for the ambiguous 10%, then back to the workflow for publish.
C) Augmented single call with no tools.
D) Manual only.</p>

<p id="sec-bank-mocks-p341" data-kind="prose"><strong>Key.</strong> B. The decisive phrase is: &quot;Steps are fixed... 10% need judgment on ambiguous events.&quot; Fixed steps need the workflow. The ambiguous 10% needs runtime judgment. The hybrid joins them.</p>

<p id="sec-bank-mocks-p342" data-kind="prose">A wins when you cannot fix the event list in advance.</p>


<h3 id="bank-mocks-h77">M2-Q06 (V2-D1.3)</h3>

<p id="sec-bank-mocks-p343" data-kind="prose"><strong>Scenario.</strong> A support bot answers from a knowledge base. One retrieval
plus one judgment resolves 95% of chats.</p>

<p id="sec-bank-mocks-p344" data-kind="prose"><strong>Question.</strong> Which pattern fits?</p>

<p id="sec-bank-mocks-p345" data-kind="prose"><strong>Options.</strong>
A) Autonomous loop with 10 steps.
B) Augmented single call: retrieve, then judge once.
C) Multi-agent coordinator.
D) Deterministic workflow with 12 steps.</p>

<p id="sec-bank-mocks-p346" data-kind="prose"><strong>Key.</strong> B. Decisive phrase: "One retrieval plus one judgment resolves
95% of chats." Why: one judgment with tool context is the augmented
call's exact shape. A loop adds iterations with no planning to do.
Counterfactual: A wins when the next step depends on the previous tool
result.</p>


<h3 id="bank-mocks-h78">M2-Q07 (V2-D1.4)</h3>

<p id="sec-bank-mocks-p347" data-kind="prose"><strong>Scenario.</strong> A code review uses three workers: lint, tests, security

<p id="sec-bank-mocks-p348" data-kind="prose"><strong>Question.</strong> What gap exists?</p>

<p id="sec-bank-mocks-p349" data-kind="prose"><strong>Options.</strong>
A) More workers.
B) The disagree rule in the handoff contract: severity order or an
arbiter, applied at merge and logged.
C) A bigger model.
D) A longer timeout.</p>

<p id="sec-bank-mocks-p350" data-kind="prose"><strong>Key.</strong> B. Decisive phrase: "no rule says which finding wins." Why:
V2-D1.4 requires the disagree rule in the contract. Parallel work
without a merge rule is just parallel arguments. Counterfactual: A
wins when the work splits into more independent pieces.</p>


<h3 id="bank-mocks-h79">M2-Q08 (V2-D1.5)</h3>

dependency. Counterfactual: C wins when churn truly derives from the
sales output.</p>


<h3 id="bank-mocks-h81">M2-Q10 (V2-D1.6)</h3>

<p id="sec-bank-mocks-p360" data-kind="prose"><strong>Scenario.</strong> A clinic automates appointment reminders. Staff spent 2 hours per day on calls. The automated design costs $60 per month. The no-show rate does not change.</p>

<p id="sec-bank-mocks-p361" data-kind="prose"><strong>Question.</strong> Which number decides the value?</p>

<p id="sec-bank-mocks-p362" data-kind="prose"><strong>Options.</strong>
A) The model's accuracy.
B) Net monthly value: (2 hours x 30 x hourly labor cost) - $60.
C) The p95 latency.
D) The number of lines of code.</p>

<p id="sec-bank-mocks-p363" data-kind="prose"><strong>Key.</strong> B. Decisive phrase: "Staff spent 2 hours per day... costs $60
per month." Why: V2-D1.6 compares baseline labor against full cost.
Time saved times labor rate, minus run cost, is the net. Counterfactual:
C wins when an SLA names latency with a penalty.</p>


<h3 id="bank-mocks-h82">M2-Q11 (V2-D1.6)</h3>

<p id="sec-bank-mocks-p364" data-kind="prose"><strong>Scenario.</strong> A team proposes cutting model cost 50% on a fraud
detector. The detector currently stops $200,000 per month in fraud. The
cut risks 2% more missed fraud.</p>

<p id="sec-bank-mocks-p365" data-kind="prose"><strong>Question.</strong> What decides?</p>

<p id="sec-bank-mocks-p366" data-kind="prose"><strong>Options.</strong>
A) The 50% cost cut alone.
B) Net value: cost saved vs expected fraud missed (2% of $200,000 =
$4,000 per month) plus quality floor effects.
C) The tier name.
D) The team's enthusiasm.</p>

<p id="sec-bank-mocks-p367" data-kind="prose"><strong>Key.</strong> B. Decisive phrase: "stops $200,000 per month... risks 2% more
missed fraud." Why: the cut's savings must beat $4,000 per month in new
losses. Business value decides. The percentage cut does not.
Counterfactual: A wins when misses cost nothing.</p>


<h3 id="bank-mocks-h83">M2-Q12 (V2-D2.1)</h3>

<p id="sec-bank-mocks-p369" data-kind="prose"><strong>Question.</strong> Which tier strategy fits?</p>

floor on the stranding-risk answers.
